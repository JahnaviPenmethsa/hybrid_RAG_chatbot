--- FEVER IN CHILDREN ---

Fever management varies significantly by age group.

Fever in Infants Under 3 Months:
⚠️ ANY fever (above 38°C) in a baby under 3 months is a medical emergency.
Call 112 or go to emergency immediately.
Do not give paracetamol without medical advice in this age group.

Fever in Children 3-36 Months:
- Fever above 39°C warrants medical evaluation
- Febrile seizures can occur (3-5% of children): brief convulsion during fever
- Febrile seizure first aid: place child on side, do not restrain, time the seizure, call 112 if lasting more than 5 minutes

Fever in Children Above 3 Years:
- Paracetamol (15mg/kg every 4-6 hours, max 4 doses/day)
- Ibuprofen (10mg/kg every 6-8 hours, from 6 months of age)
- Do not use aspirin in children (risk of Reye's syndrome)
- Tepid sponging for comfort (not alcohol)
- Ensure adequate hydration

Seek emergency care if:
- Fever above 40°C
- Stiff neck
- Skin rash (especially non-blanching purple/red spots — meningococcal disease)
- Difficulty breathing
- Extreme lethargy or difficulty waking
- Severe headache
- Persistent vomiting

--- COMMON CHILDHOOD ILLNESSES ---

Acute Otitis Media (Ear Infection):
- Common in children under 5 years
- Symptoms: ear pain (child may tug or rub ear), fever, irritability, difficulty hearing, sometimes discharge from ear
- Treatment: paracetamol for pain, antibiotics if bacterial (amoxicillin first line)

Croup:
- Viral infection causing swelling around vocal cords
- Symptoms: barking cough (seal-like), hoarse voice, stridor (high-pitched sound when breathing in), low fever
- Management: cool mist humidifier, oral dexamethasone, nebulised adrenaline for severe cases
- ⚠️ Severe stridor at rest or respiratory distress = emergency

Bronchiolitis:
- Viral infection of small airways, most common in infants under 2 years
- Caused mainly by RSV (Respiratory Syncytial Virus)
- Symptoms: runny nose progressing to wheeze and increased breathing effort, fever, poor feeding
- Management: supportive (oxygen if needed, fluids), no antibiotics
- ⚠️ Hospital admission if oxygen saturation below 92%, unable to feed, severe respiratory distress

Hand, Foot and Mouth Disease:
- Viral illness in young children
- Symptoms: fever, sores in mouth, rash with blisters on hands, feet, and buttocks
- Management: symptomatic, paracetamol, ensure hydration
- Highly contagious — keep child away from school until fever-free and blisters dried

Rotavirus Diarrhea:
- Leading cause of severe diarrhea in children under 5 years
- Prevention: Rotavirus vaccine (part of India's national immunisation programme)
- Treatment: ORS is the cornerstone of management

--- CHILDHOOD VACCINATIONS (India National Immunisation Programme) ---

Birth: BCG, Hepatitis B, OPV (oral polio vaccine)
6 weeks: DTP, IPV, Hib, Hepatitis B, OPV, Rotavirus, PCV
10 weeks: DTP, IPV, Hib, Hepatitis B, OPV, Rotavirus, PCV
14 weeks: DTP, IPV, Hib, Hepatitis B, OPV, Rotavirus, PCV
6 months: Influenza (annual)
9 months: Measles-Rubella (MR), OPV
12 months: Hepatitis A (1st dose)
15 months: MMR, Varicella, PCV booster
18 months: DTP booster, IPV booster, Hib booster, Hepatitis A (2nd dose)
2 years: Typhoid conjugate vaccine
5 years: DTP booster
10 and 16 years: Td booster

⚠️ Ensure your child's vaccinations are up to date. Vaccination cards should be maintained carefully.

--- MALNUTRITION IN CHILDREN ---

Malnutrition remains a significant problem in India, affecting child growth and development.

Signs of malnutrition:
- Severe acute malnutrition (SAM): very low weight-for-height, visible wasting, oedema
- Moderate acute malnutrition (MAM): low weight-for-height
- Stunting: low height-for-age (chronic malnutrition)

Warning signs requiring immediate attention:
- Bilateral pitting oedema (swelling of both feet — kwashiorkor)
- Very severe wasting
- Inability to eat or drink

Management: therapeutic feeding (RUTF — ready-to-use therapeutic food), treatment of infections, micronutrient supplementation.

--- HEALTH CONCERNS IN THE ELDERLY ---

Falls and Fall Prevention:
Falls are the leading cause of injury in older adults. Major risk factors: muscle weakness, balance problems, vision changes, medications (sedatives, blood pressure drugs), environmental hazards.

Consequences: hip fractures (high mortality in elderly), head injury, loss of confidence and independence.

Prevention:
- Exercise: strength and balance training (Tai Chi proven effective)
- Home modifications: remove rugs, install grab bars, ensure good lighting
- Medication review: reduce sedating medications
- Vision correction
- Calcium and vitamin D supplementation

Dementia and Alzheimer's Disease:
- Progressive loss of memory and cognitive function
- Early symptoms: forgetting recent events, difficulty finding words, getting lost in familiar places, personality changes
- Diagnosis: clinical assessment, neuropsychological testing, CT or MRI brain
- Management: no cure — cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil) for mild-moderate, carer support, safety planning

Confusion in the Elderly (Delirium):
- Acute confusion is a medical emergency in elderly patients
- Causes: infection (UTI, pneumonia most common), medication side effects, metabolic disturbance, pain
- ⚠️ New confusion in an elderly person requires urgent medical evaluation

Osteoporosis:
- Loss of bone density, increasing fracture risk
- Often silent until fracture occurs (wrist, spine, hip)
- Diagnosis: DEXA scan (bone mineral density)
- Treatment: calcium (1000-1200mg/day), vitamin D (800-1000 IU/day), bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate)
- Prevention: weight-bearing exercise, adequate calcium and vitamin D throughout life

Polypharmacy (Multiple Medications):
- Common in elderly with multiple conditions
- Increases risk of drug interactions and side effects
- Regular medication review by doctor is essential
- Never stop or change medications without medical advice
