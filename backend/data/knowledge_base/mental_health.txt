--- DEPRESSION (MAJOR DEPRESSIVE DISORDER) ---

Depression is a common and serious medical condition that negatively affects how a person feels, thinks, and acts. It is not a sign of weakness and is highly treatable. India has one of the highest burdens of depression globally.

Symptoms (present for at least 2 weeks):
- Persistent sadness, emptiness, or hopelessness
- Loss of interest or pleasure in activities once enjoyed (anhedonia)
- Significant changes in appetite (weight loss or gain)
- Sleep disturbances: insomnia or sleeping too much
- Fatigue and loss of energy
- Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
- Difficulty thinking, concentrating, or making decisions
- Psychomotor changes: moving or speaking more slowly, or being restless
- Recurrent thoughts of death or suicide

Severity levels:
- Mild: symptoms present but person can function
- Moderate: symptoms significantly affect daily functioning
- Severe: symptoms severely impair daily life, may include psychosis

When to seek help immediately:
⚠️ If experiencing thoughts of self-harm or suicide, seek help immediately. Call iCall (9152987821) or Vandrevala Foundation (1860-2662-345, 24/7).

Treatment:
- Psychotherapy: Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT), interpersonal therapy
- Antidepressants: SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram) — take 4-6 weeks to show effect
- Combination of therapy and medication is most effective
- Lifestyle: regular exercise, sleep hygiene, social support
- Never stop antidepressants abruptly without doctor guidance

--- ANXIETY DISORDERS ---

Anxiety disorders are the most common mental health conditions worldwide. They involve excessive fear or worry that interferes with daily life.

Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD):
- Excessive, uncontrollable worry about multiple areas of life
- Restlessness or feeling on edge
- Fatigue
- Difficulty concentrating
- Irritability
- Muscle tension
- Sleep disturbances

Panic Disorder:
Recurrent unexpected panic attacks — intense episodes of fear peaking within minutes.
Symptoms during panic attack:
- Rapid heartbeat (palpitations)
- Chest pain or tightness
- Shortness of breath
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Sweating and trembling
- Nausea
- Feeling of choking
- Numbness or tingling
- Chills or hot flushes
- Fear of losing control or dying

Note: Panic attacks are not dangerous but can feel like a heart attack. If first episode, seek medical evaluation to rule out cardiac cause.

Social Anxiety Disorder:
- Intense fear of social situations and being judged or embarrassed
- Avoidance of social situations
- Physical symptoms in social settings: blushing, sweating, trembling

Treatment:
- CBT (most effective)
- Exposure therapy
- SSRIs and SNRIs
- Short-term benzodiazepines for acute anxiety (caution: dependence risk)
- Relaxation techniques, mindfulness, deep breathing

--- STRESS AND BURNOUT ---

Stress is the body's response to demands that exceed perceived resources. Chronic stress leads to burnout and physical health problems.

Symptoms of chronic stress:
- Persistent headaches
- Muscle tension and pain (especially neck and shoulders)
- Fatigue despite adequate sleep
- Sleep problems: difficulty falling asleep or staying asleep
- Digestive problems: nausea, diarrhea, stomach ache
- Chest tightness
- Irritability and mood changes
- Difficulty concentrating
- Withdrawal from social activities
- Increased use of alcohol, tobacco, or caffeine

Burnout (work-related exhaustion):
- Emotional exhaustion: feeling emotionally drained and depleted
- Depersonalization: cynicism, detachment from work or people
- Reduced personal accomplishment: feeling ineffective and incompetent

Management:
- Identify and reduce stressors where possible
- Time management and setting boundaries
- Regular physical exercise (reduces cortisol)
- Mindfulness and meditation (proven to reduce anxiety)
- Adequate sleep: 7-9 hours per night
- Social support: talk to trusted friends or family
- Professional help if stress is persistent or severe

--- INSOMNIA AND SLEEP DISORDERS ---

Insomnia is difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early, causing daytime impairment.

Symptoms:
- Difficulty falling asleep at night
- Waking up during the night
- Waking up too early
- Not feeling rested after sleep
- Daytime tiredness or sleepiness
- Difficulty concentrating
- Irritability, depression, or anxiety
- Errors or accidents due to fatigue

Types:
- Acute insomnia: brief, often caused by stress or a life event
- Chronic insomnia: occurs at least 3 nights per week for 3 months or more

Sleep Hygiene (first-line treatment):
- Maintain a consistent sleep schedule (same time every day including weekends)
- Keep bedroom cool, dark, and quiet
- Avoid screens 1 hour before bed (blue light suppresses melatonin)
- Avoid caffeine after 2pm
- Avoid large meals and alcohol close to bedtime
- Regular daytime exercise (but not within 3 hours of sleep)
- Use bed only for sleep (not work or TV)

Treatment:
- Cognitive Behavioural Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I): most effective long-term treatment
- Short-term sleep aids: melatonin (0.5-5mg), antihistamines
- Prescription medications (short-term only): benzodiazepines, Z-drugs (zolpidem)

--- GRIEF AND ADJUSTMENT DISORDERS ---

Grief is a natural response to loss. Adjustment disorder occurs when stress or life changes cause emotional or behavioural symptoms beyond what is expected.

Normal grief symptoms:
- Sadness and crying
- Shock and disbelief
- Anger or guilt
- Anxiety about the future
- Physical symptoms: fatigue, sleep disturbance, appetite changes

Complicated grief (prolonged grief disorder):
- Intense longing for the deceased that does not diminish over time
- Difficulty accepting the loss
- Bitterness or anger about the loss
- Feeling that life is meaningless without the deceased

Treatment: Psychotherapy, support groups, time. Medication if depression develops.

--- WHEN TO SEEK PROFESSIONAL HELP ---

Seek mental health support if:
- Symptoms persist for more than 2 weeks
- Symptoms significantly affect work, relationships, or daily life
- You are using substances to cope
- You have thoughts of harming yourself or others
