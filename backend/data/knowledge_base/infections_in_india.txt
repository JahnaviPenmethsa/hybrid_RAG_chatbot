--- TUBERCULOSIS (TB) ---

Tuberculosis is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. India has the highest TB burden in the world, accounting for approximately 26% of global cases.

Pulmonary TB (lung TB) Symptoms:
- Persistent cough lasting 2 weeks or more (may be productive with mucus or blood)
- Coughing up blood (haemoptysis)
- Chest pain
- Fever (low-grade, often in the afternoons)
- Night sweats
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fatigue and weakness
- Loss of appetite

Extra-pulmonary TB (TB in other organs):
- TB meningitis: headache, fever, neck stiffness, confusion
- TB lymphadenitis: swollen, non-tender lymph nodes in neck
- TB spine (Pott's disease): back pain, neurological symptoms
- TB abdomen: abdominal pain, distension, ascites

Diagnosis:
- Sputum smear microscopy (CBNAAT/GeneXpert — most sensitive)
- Chest X-ray
- Mantoux test (tuberculin skin test)
- IGRA blood test (QuantiFERON)
- CT scan for extrapulmonary TB

Treatment (DOTS — Directly Observed Treatment, Short Course):
- Intensive phase (2 months): Isoniazid + Rifampicin + Pyrazinamide + Ethambutol
- Continuation phase (4 months): Isoniazid + Rifampicin
- Drug-resistant TB (MDR-TB): longer regimens with second-line drugs
- Treatment is FREE under India's National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP)

⚠️ Never stop TB treatment midway — incomplete treatment causes drug resistance.

Isolation: Pulmonary TB is infectious until 2 weeks of treatment. Use masks and improve ventilation.

--- CHOLERA AND ACUTE WATERY DIARRHEA ---

Cholera is caused by Vibrio cholerae, spread through contaminated water and food. Endemic in parts of India, especially during monsoon.

Symptoms:
- Profuse, watery diarrhea (rice-water stools)
- Rapid dehydration
- Vomiting
- Muscle cramps
- Weakness
- Rapid heart rate
- Low blood pressure

⚠️ Severe dehydration can be fatal within hours — immediate treatment is essential.

Treatment:
- Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS): critical first step
- IV fluids for severe dehydration
- Antibiotics (doxycycline or azithromycin) to shorten duration

Prevention:
- Safe drinking water (boil or use water purification tablets)
- Safe food handling
- Handwashing with soap after toilet use and before eating
- Cholera vaccine available for high-risk areas

--- LEPTOSPIROSIS ---

Bacterial infection (Leptospira) spread through water or soil contaminated with animal urine. Common during and after monsoon in India, particularly in urban flooding areas.

Symptoms (appear 2-30 days after exposure):
- Sudden high fever with chills
- Severe headache
- Muscle pain (especially calves)
- Red eyes (conjunctival suffusion)
- Nausea and vomiting
- Skin rash (sometimes)

Severe leptospirosis (Weil's disease):
- Jaundice (yellow skin and eyes)
- Kidney failure (oliguria, dark urine)
- Bleeding (from gums, into skin)
- Lung involvement: cough, shortness of breath
- Meningitis

⚠️ Weil's disease is a medical emergency requiring hospitalisation.

Diagnosis: blood culture, serology (MAT test), urine culture.
Treatment: Penicillin, doxycycline, or ceftriaxone (IV for severe cases).

Prevention: avoid wading in floodwater, wear protective footwear, doxycycline prophylaxis in high-risk settings.

--- SCRUB TYPHUS ---

Bacterial infection (Orientia tsutsugamushi) transmitted by chigger mite bites. Increasingly common in India, often misdiagnosed.

Symptoms:
- Fever (high, sudden onset)
- Headache
- Body aches
- Characteristic eschar (black, painless scab at bite site — look in hidden areas: armpits, groin, behind ears)
- Rash (maculopapular, appears 5-8 days after fever)
- Swollen lymph nodes

Complications: pneumonia, meningitis, hepatitis, myocarditis.

Diagnosis: Weil-Felix test (insensitive), IgM ELISA for Orientia, PCR.
Treatment: Doxycycline 100mg twice daily for 7-14 days. Fever resolves dramatically within 24-48 hours of treatment (diagnostic response).

--- VIRAL FEVER ---

A broad category of febrile illnesses caused by various viruses. Very common in India, especially during seasonal changes and monsoon.

General symptoms:
- Sudden onset fever (38-40°C)
- Headache
- Body aches and joint pain
- Fatigue and weakness
- Sore throat
- Runny nose or nasal congestion
- Redness of eyes
- Rash (in some viral fevers)
- Nausea (sometimes)

Duration: typically 3-7 days.

Management:
- Rest and adequate hydration
- Paracetamol for fever and pain
- Avoid aspirin and ibuprofen (dengue cannot be excluded initially)
- Seek medical care if: fever persists beyond 3 days, rash develops, severe headache, bleeding signs, platelet count drops

Differentiating common viral fevers in India:
- Dengue: retro-orbital pain, rash day 2-5, low platelet count
- Chikungunya: severe joint pain (arthralgia), rash, joint swelling
- Influenza: respiratory symptoms predominate, sudden onset, severe body aches
- Scrub typhus: eschar, lymphadenopathy
- Malaria: cyclical fever pattern, chills and rigors

--- CHIKUNGUNYA ---

Viral infection transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes. Endemic in India.

Symptoms:
- Sudden high fever
- Severe joint pain and swelling (can be debilitating — affects multiple joints)
- Muscle pain
- Headache
- Skin rash (maculopapular, 2-5 days after fever)
- Fatigue
- Conjunctivitis

Joint pain may persist for months to years (post-chikungunya arthritis).

Treatment (symptomatic only, no antiviral):
- Paracetamol for fever and pain
- NSAIDs for joint pain (once dengue excluded)
- Rest and hydration
- Physiotherapy for persistent joint pain

Prevention: mosquito control measures (same as dengue).

--- COVID-19 (Updated Guidance) ---

COVID-19 remains circulating with various variants. Most current infections are milder due to widespread immunity.

Common symptoms (Omicron and variants):
- Sore throat (often prominent)
- Runny nose
- Fatigue
- Headache
- Mild fever
- Cough (often dry)
- Body aches
- Loss of taste or smell (less common with Omicron)

High-risk groups for severe disease:
- Age above 60 years
- Unvaccinated individuals
- Diabetes, heart disease, chronic lung disease
- Immunocompromised individuals

Emergency warning signs:
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Persistent chest pain or pressure
- Confusion or inability to wake up
- Oxygen saturation below 94% (use pulse oximeter)
- Bluish lips or face

Prevention:
- COVID-19 vaccination (including booster doses)
- Mask in crowded enclosed spaces
- Hand hygiene
- Ventilation of indoor spaces

Isolation: Current Indian guidelines — isolate for 5 days if testing positive. Wear mask for 10 days.
