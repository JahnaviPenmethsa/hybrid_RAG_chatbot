--- MENSTRUAL DISORDERS ---

Dysmenorrhea (Painful Periods):
Painful menstrual cramps occurring before or during menstruation. Very common — affects up to 80% of women at some point.

Primary dysmenorrhea: No underlying disease — caused by prostaglandins causing uterine contractions.
Secondary dysmenorrhea: Due to underlying conditions (endometriosis, fibroids, pelvic inflammatory disease).

Symptoms:
- Cramping pain in lower abdomen, often radiating to lower back and thighs
- Pain starting 1-2 days before period and lasting 2-3 days
- Nausea and vomiting
- Diarrhoea or loose stools
- Headache
- Fatigue
- Dizziness

Management:
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen 400mg, mefenamic acid): start 1-2 days before period, most effective
- Heat therapy: hot water bottle on lower abdomen
- Exercise: regular physical activity reduces severity
- Oral contraceptive pills (reduce severity in primary dysmenorrhea)
- Investigation for secondary causes if severe or not responding to treatment

Menorrhagia (Heavy Periods):
Abnormally heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding.

Definition: Soaking a pad or tampon every hour for several consecutive hours, or periods lasting more than 7 days.

Causes: fibroids, adenomyosis, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), thyroid disorders, bleeding disorders, intrauterine device (IUD).

Symptoms:
- Heavy flow requiring frequent pad or tampon changes
- Passing large blood clots
- Prolonged periods (more than 7 days)
- Anaemia symptoms: fatigue, shortness of breath, pallor
- Pelvic pain

Investigation: pelvic ultrasound, CBC, thyroid function, coagulation tests.

Amenorrhea (Absent Periods):
- Primary: no periods by age 16 — requires evaluation
- Secondary: previously regular periods stop for 3 or more months (excluding pregnancy)
- Causes: pregnancy, PCOS, thyroid disorders, hyperprolactinaemia, excessive exercise, low body weight, stress

--- POLYCYSTIC OVARY SYNDROME (PCOS) ---

PCOS is one of the most common hormonal disorders in women of reproductive age. India has a very high prevalence (approximately 20-25% of women).

Features (Rotterdam criteria — 2 of 3):
- Irregular or absent periods (oligo/anovulation)
- Excess androgen (male hormones): symptoms or lab evidence
- Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound

Symptoms:
- Irregular periods (fewer than 8 periods per year)
- Excess facial and body hair (hirsutism)
- Acne
- Thinning hair or hair loss (scalp)
- Weight gain, particularly around the abdomen
- Skin darkening in body folds (acanthosis nigricans)
- Difficulty getting pregnant (infertility)

Associated risks:
- Type 2 diabetes (insulin resistance is central to PCOS)
- Cardiovascular disease
- Depression and anxiety
- Endometrial cancer (from irregular periods without ovulation)

Management:
- Weight loss: even 5-10% weight reduction significantly improves symptoms
- Lifestyle: regular exercise, low glycaemic index diet
- Oral contraceptive pills: regulate periods and reduce androgen symptoms
- Metformin: improves insulin resistance
- Anti-androgens (spironolactone) for hirsutism and acne
- Ovulation induction for fertility (clomiphene, letrozole)

--- PREGNANCY-RELATED CONDITIONS ---

Signs of Normal Pregnancy:
- Missed period
- Breast tenderness
- Nausea (morning sickness)
- Fatigue
- Frequent urination
- Food cravings or aversions

Warning Signs During Pregnancy (seek immediate care):
⚠️ Vaginal bleeding at any stage
⚠️ Severe abdominal pain or cramping
⚠️ Severe headache with visual disturbance (pre-eclampsia)
⚠️ Swelling of face and hands (pre-eclampsia)
⚠️ Decreased fetal movement after 28 weeks
⚠️ Signs of preterm labour: regular contractions before 37 weeks
⚠️ Fever above 38°C
⚠️ Burning urination (UTI, common in pregnancy)

Gestational Diabetes:
- Occurs in 10-20% of Indian pregnancies
- Screening: oral glucose tolerance test at 24-28 weeks
- Management: diet, exercise, insulin if needed
- Increases risk of Type 2 diabetes later in life

Pre-eclampsia:
- High blood pressure (above 140/90) after 20 weeks with protein in urine
- Symptoms: severe headache, visual changes, upper abdominal pain, swelling
- ⚠️ EMERGENCY: Can lead to seizures (eclampsia) — requires immediate hospitalisation

--- MENOPAUSE ---

Menopause is defined as 12 consecutive months without a menstrual period, typically occurring between ages 45-55.

Perimenopause symptoms (years before menopause):
- Irregular periods
- Hot flushes (sudden feelings of warmth, especially in face and upper body)
- Night sweats
- Sleep disturbances
- Mood changes: irritability, anxiety, depression
- Vaginal dryness
- Decreased libido
- Difficulty concentrating (brain fog)
- Joint pain

Long-term health risks after menopause:
- Osteoporosis: bone density loss, increased fracture risk
- Cardiovascular disease: oestrogen was protective
- Urinary incontinence

Management:
- Hormone replacement therapy (HRT): most effective for hot flushes and vaginal symptoms
- Non-hormonal options: SSRIs, clonidine for hot flushes
- Calcium and vitamin D supplementation for bone health
- Weight-bearing exercise
- Vaginal moisturisers and lubricants for vaginal dryness

--- BREAST HEALTH ---

Breast Self-Examination (BSE):
Recommended monthly, 1 week after period.
Look for: new lumps, change in size or shape, skin dimpling, nipple changes or discharge, redness or rash.

⚠️ Any new breast lump should be evaluated by a doctor — most are benign but require assessment.

Breast conditions:
- Fibrocystic changes: common benign lumps that fluctuate with menstrual cycle
- Fibroadenoma: common benign tumour, smooth and mobile
- Breast infection (mastitis): common in breastfeeding women — pain, redness, warmth, fever
- Breast cancer: persistent lump, skin changes, nipple inversion or discharge, axillary lymph node enlargement

Risk factors for breast cancer: family history, BRCA gene mutations, early menarche, late menopause, hormone therapy, alcohol, obesity.

Screening: Clinical breast examination annually from age 40; mammography every 1-2 years from age 40-50 onwards.

--- PELVIC INFLAMMATORY DISEASE (PID) ---

Infection of the female reproductive organs (uterus, fallopian tubes, ovaries).

Symptoms:
- Lower abdominal and pelvic pain (can be mild to severe)
- Fever and chills
- Unusual or heavy vaginal discharge with odour
- Painful intercourse
- Painful or frequent urination
- Irregular bleeding

⚠️ Untreated PID can lead to infertility, ectopic pregnancy, and chronic pelvic pain.

Treatment: Antibiotics (combination therapy — doxycycline + metronidazole or ceftriaxone). Hospitalisation for severe cases.
