--- TYPE 1 DIABETES ---

Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition where the pancreas produces little or no insulin. It can occur at any age but is most common in children and young adults.

Symptoms (often sudden onset):
- Excessive thirst (polydipsia)
- Frequent urination (polyuria)
- Unintended weight loss
- Extreme hunger (polyphagia)
- Fatigue and weakness
- Blurred vision
- Fruity-smelling breath (sign of diabetic ketoacidosis)
- Irritability and mood changes

Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) — Emergency:
DKA is a life-threatening complication of Type 1 diabetes.
Signs: fruity breath, vomiting, abdominal pain, rapid breathing, confusion, loss of consciousness.
⚠️ EMERGENCY: Call 112 immediately if DKA is suspected.

Management:
- Insulin therapy (multiple daily injections or insulin pump)
- Regular blood glucose monitoring
- Carbohydrate counting and meal planning
- HbA1c target: below 7% (ICMR guidelines)

--- TYPE 2 DIABETES ---

Type 2 diabetes is the most common form, characterized by insulin resistance. India has one of the highest burdens of Type 2 diabetes globally.

Symptoms (often gradual, may be silent for years):
- Increased thirst and frequent urination
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing wounds or frequent infections
- Tingling or numbness in hands and feet (peripheral neuropathy)
- Darkened skin in body creases (acanthosis nigricans)
- Unexplained weight gain or loss

Risk Factors in India:
- Family history of diabetes
- Overweight or obesity (especially central/abdominal obesity)
- Physical inactivity
- Age above 40 years
- History of gestational diabetes
- High blood pressure or abnormal cholesterol

Diagnosis:
- Fasting blood glucose: 126 mg/dL or higher (on two occasions)
- HbA1c: 6.5% or higher
- Random blood glucose: 200 mg/dL or higher with symptoms
- Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT): 2-hour value 200 mg/dL or higher

Management:
- Lifestyle modification: diet, exercise (150 minutes/week moderate activity)
- Oral medications: metformin (first line), sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors
- Insulin therapy when oral medications are insufficient
- Regular monitoring: HbA1c every 3 months, annual eye and kidney check
- Blood pressure control (target below 130/80 mmHg)
- Cholesterol management (statin therapy if indicated)

--- HYPOGLYCEMIA (LOW BLOOD SUGAR) ---

Blood glucose below 70 mg/dL. Can occur in diabetic patients on insulin or certain oral medications.

Symptoms:
- Shakiness and trembling
- Sweating
- Rapid heartbeat (palpitations)
- Dizziness and lightheadedness
- Confusion or difficulty concentrating
- Headache
- Irritability or anxiety
- Hunger
- Pale skin
- Blurred vision

Severe hypoglycemia: Loss of consciousness or seizure.
⚠️ EMERGENCY if unconscious — do not give anything by mouth, call 112.

Treatment of mild to moderate hypoglycemia (15-15 rule):
- Take 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate (4 glucose tablets, 150mL fruit juice, 3 teaspoons of sugar)
- Wait 15 minutes and recheck blood glucose
- Repeat if still below 70 mg/dL
- Follow with a snack or meal

--- OBESITY AND METABOLIC SYNDROME ---

Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of conditions increasing risk of heart disease, stroke, and Type 2 diabetes.

Criteria (any 3 of 5):
- Waist circumference above 90cm (men) or 80cm (women) — Indian-specific cutoffs
- Triglycerides 150 mg/dL or higher
- HDL cholesterol below 40 mg/dL (men) or 50 mg/dL (women)
- Blood pressure 130/85 mmHg or higher
- Fasting glucose 100 mg/dL or higher

Management:
- Weight loss of 5-10% significantly reduces risk
- Low glycaemic index diet
- Regular aerobic exercise
- Smoking cessation
- Reduce alcohol consumption

--- THYROID DISORDERS ---

Hypothyroidism (Underactive thyroid):
Symptoms: fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, hair loss, depression, slow heart rate, muscle weakness, puffy face.
Treatment: Levothyroxine (daily oral tablet).

Hyperthyroidism (Overactive thyroid):
Symptoms: weight loss despite increased appetite, rapid or irregular heartbeat, anxiety, tremors, excessive sweating, heat intolerance, frequent bowel movements, bulging eyes (Graves disease).
Treatment: Antithyroid medications (methimazole), radioiodine therapy, or surgery.

Thyroid Storm — Emergency:
Extreme hyperthyroidism with fever, rapid heart rate, confusion.
⚠️ EMERGENCY: Requires immediate hospitalization.

Diagnosis: TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone), Free T4, Free T3 blood tests.

--- DIABETES COMPLICATIONS ---

Long-term complications of poorly controlled diabetes:

Diabetic Nephropathy (Kidney damage):
- Symptoms: protein in urine, swelling in legs and feet, high blood pressure, fatigue
- Monitoring: annual urine microalbumin test, serum creatinine

Diabetic Retinopathy (Eye damage):
- Symptoms: blurred vision, floaters, gradual vision loss
- Monitoring: annual dilated eye examination

Diabetic Neuropathy (Nerve damage):
- Symptoms: tingling, numbness, burning pain in feet and hands, loss of sensation
- Risk: foot ulcers and infections due to reduced sensation

Diabetic Foot:
- Any wound on a diabetic foot requires prompt medical attention
- Risk of infection, gangrene, and amputation if untreated
- Daily foot inspection is essential

Cardiovascular Disease:
- Diabetics have 2-4 times higher risk of heart attack and stroke
- Aggressive management of blood pressure, cholesterol, and glucose is essential
