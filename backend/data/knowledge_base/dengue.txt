--- DENGUE FEVER ---

Dengue is a mosquito-borne viral infection caused by the dengue virus (DENV), transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes, particularly Aedes aegypti.

Dengue is endemic in India and prevalent during and after monsoon season.

Symptoms of Dengue (Classic):
- Sudden high fever (39–40°C) lasting 2–7 days
- Severe headache (especially behind the eyes — retro-orbital pain)
- Joint pain and muscle pain (dengue is called "breakbone fever")
- Body aches
- Skin rash (appears 2–5 days after fever onset — flat red rash)
- Nausea and vomiting
- Fatigue

Warning Signs of Severe Dengue (Seek Emergency Care Immediately):
- Abdominal pain or tenderness
- Persistent vomiting (3 or more times in 24 hours)
- Bleeding from gums, nose, or in vomit/stool
- Rapid breathing
- Fatigue and restlessness
- Blood in urine, stool, or vomit
- Platelet count falling rapidly

Diagnosis:
- NS1 antigen test (positive in first 5 days)
- Dengue IgM/IgG antibody test (after 5 days)
- Complete Blood Count (CBC) — platelet and WBC count

Treatment (No specific antiviral):
- Strict bed rest
- Oral rehydration — drink at least 2–3 liters of water/ORS daily
- Paracetamol for fever (500–1000mg every 6 hours for adults)
- AVOID: Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Diclofenac (increase bleeding risk)
- Monitor platelet count daily from day 3 of fever
- Hospitalization if platelet < 50,000/mm³ or warning signs present

Prevention:
- Eliminate mosquito breeding sites (stagnant water)
- Use mosquito repellents and nets
- Wear long-sleeved clothing
- Community fogging programs

Key Differentiating Features from Flu:
- Retro-orbital eye pain
- Rash appearance after 2–4 days
- Thrombocytopenia (low platelet count)
- More severe joint/muscle pain than flu
