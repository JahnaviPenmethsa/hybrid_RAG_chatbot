--- CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE (CKD) ---

Chronic kidney disease is the gradual loss of kidney function over months or years. India has a very high burden of CKD, largely driven by diabetes and hypertension.

Stages of CKD (based on GFR — glomerular filtration rate):
- Stage 1: GFR 90+ (kidney damage with normal function)
- Stage 2: GFR 60-89 (mildly reduced)
- Stage 3: GFR 30-59 (moderately reduced)
- Stage 4: GFR 15-29 (severely reduced)
- Stage 5: GFR below 15 (kidney failure — dialysis or transplant required)

Symptoms (often absent in early stages):
- Fatigue and weakness
- Swelling of ankles, feet, and legs (oedema)
- Shortness of breath (fluid in lungs)
- Decreased urine output or changes in urination
- Foamy or bubbly urine (protein in urine)
- Blood in urine (haematuria)
- Persistent itching (due to waste buildup)
- Nausea and vomiting
- Loss of appetite
- Muscle cramps
- Difficulty concentrating
- High blood pressure

Causes in India:
- Diabetic nephropathy (most common)
- Hypertensive nephropathy
- Glomerulonephritis
- Polycystic kidney disease
- Recurrent urinary tract infections

Management:
- Control blood pressure: target below 130/80 mmHg (ACE inhibitors or ARBs preferred)
- Control blood sugar in diabetics
- Low protein diet (0.6-0.8 g/kg/day in advanced CKD)
- Low potassium and phosphorus diet in advanced stages
- Avoid NSAIDs and nephrotoxic medications
- Annual kidney function tests (serum creatinine, eGFR, urine protein)
- Dialysis or kidney transplant in Stage 5

--- URINARY TRACT INFECTIONS (UTI) ---

UTIs are among the most common infections, particularly in women. They can affect the bladder (cystitis) or kidneys (pyelonephritis).

Symptoms of lower UTI (bladder infection):
- Burning or painful urination (dysuria)
- Frequent urge to urinate with little output
- Cloudy or strong-smelling urine
- Blood in urine (haematuria)
- Pelvic pain or pressure
- Low-grade fever

Symptoms of upper UTI (kidney infection — pyelonephritis):
- High fever with chills and rigors
- Severe back or flank pain (below the ribs, on one or both sides)
- Nausea and vomiting
- General feeling of illness

⚠️ Kidney infection requires prompt medical attention to prevent permanent kidney damage.

Treatment:
- Lower UTI: oral antibiotics for 3-7 days (trimethoprim, nitrofurantoin, ciprofloxacin)
- Upper UTI: oral or IV antibiotics for 7-14 days
- Increase fluid intake to flush bacteria
- Cranberry juice may help prevent recurrence (not proven as treatment)
- Avoid holding urine for long periods

Prevention:
- Wipe front to back after toilet use
- Urinate after sexual intercourse
- Stay well hydrated
- Avoid harsh soaps in genital area
- Change sanitary products frequently during menstruation

--- KIDNEY STONES (NEPHROLITHIASIS) ---

Hard deposits made of minerals and salts that form inside kidneys. Very common in India, particularly in the hot climate regions.

Symptoms (when stone moves into ureter):
- Severe, cramping pain in the back or side (renal colic) — often described as the worst pain experienced
- Pain that radiates to the lower abdomen and groin
- Pain that comes in waves and fluctuates in intensity
- Nausea and vomiting
- Blood in urine (pink, red, or brown)
- Frequent, painful urination
- Fever and chills if infection present

Treatment:
- Small stones (below 5mm): increased fluid intake (2-3 litres/day), pain relief (diclofenac), most pass naturally
- Larger stones: medical expulsive therapy (tamsulosin), lithotripsy, ureteroscopy, or surgery
- Pain management: NSAIDs are first choice for renal colic

Prevention:
- Drink 2.5-3 litres of water daily
- Reduce salt and protein intake
- Limit oxalate-rich foods (spinach, nuts, tea) for calcium oxalate stones

--- LIVER DISEASE ---

Hepatitis (Liver Inflammation):
Caused by viruses, alcohol, medications, or autoimmune conditions.

Hepatitis A:
- Spread via contaminated food and water
- Symptoms: fever, fatigue, nausea, abdominal pain, dark urine, jaundice
- Usually self-limiting, no specific treatment
- Prevention: hepatitis A vaccine, safe water and food

Hepatitis B:
- Spread via blood, sexual contact, mother to child
- Can become chronic, leading to cirrhosis and liver cancer
- Symptoms: fatigue, jaundice, abdominal pain, nausea (acute); often asymptomatic in chronic
- Treatment: antiviral medications (tenofovir, entecavir)
- Prevention: hepatitis B vaccine (part of India's national immunization programme)

Hepatitis C:
- Spread primarily via blood-to-blood contact
- Often asymptomatic for years
- Leads to chronic liver disease, cirrhosis, and liver cancer
- Treatment: direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) — highly effective cure in most cases

Alcoholic Liver Disease:
- Fatty liver: early, reversible with abstinence
- Alcoholic hepatitis: inflammation, may be severe
- Cirrhosis: irreversible scarring of the liver

Symptoms of advanced liver disease:
- Jaundice (yellow skin and eyes)
- Ascites (fluid accumulation in abdomen, causing distension)
- Portal hypertension
- Hepatic encephalopathy: confusion, altered consciousness due to toxin buildup
- Oesophageal varices: risk of severe bleeding

--- JAUNDICE ---

Yellowing of skin and eyes due to elevated bilirubin (above 2 mg/dL).

Causes:
- Pre-hepatic: haemolysis (malaria, sickle cell disease)
- Hepatic: hepatitis, cirrhosis, alcoholic liver disease
- Post-hepatic (obstructive): gallstones, pancreatic cancer, bile duct obstruction

Key diagnostic tests:
- Liver function tests (LFT): AST, ALT, ALP, bilirubin, albumin
- Ultrasound abdomen
- Hepatitis serology (HBsAg, anti-HCV, anti-HAV IgM)
- Complete blood count (CBC)

⚠️ All cases of jaundice require medical evaluation — it is always a sign of underlying disease.

Emergency signs in jaundice:
- Confusion or altered consciousness (hepatic encephalopathy)
- Severe abdominal pain
- Vomiting blood
- Rapid deterioration
