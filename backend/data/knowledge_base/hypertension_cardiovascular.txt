--- HYPERTENSION (HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE) ---

Hypertension is defined as persistent blood pressure at or above 140/90 mmHg. It is called the "silent killer" because it often has no symptoms until serious damage has occurred. India has a very high burden of hypertension with over 200 million affected adults.

Blood Pressure Classification (Indian Guidelines):
- Normal: below 120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: 120-129 / below 80 mmHg
- Stage 1 Hypertension: 130-139 / 80-89 mmHg
- Stage 2 Hypertension: 140/90 mmHg or higher
- Hypertensive Crisis: above 180/120 mmHg — requires immediate care

Symptoms (usually absent — hypertension is mostly asymptomatic):
- Severe headache (especially in the back of the head, early morning)
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Nosebleeds (epistaxis)
- Shortness of breath
- Blurred or double vision
- Chest pain or tightness
- Palpitations
- Fatigue

Note: Most people with hypertension have NO symptoms. Regular blood pressure screening is essential.

Risk Factors:
- Age (above 40 years)
- Family history of hypertension or heart disease
- Obesity and overweight
- High salt diet (India has very high dietary salt intake)
- Physical inactivity
- Smoking and tobacco use
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Stress and psychological factors
- Diabetes and kidney disease

--- HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCY ---

Blood pressure above 180/120 mmHg with signs of organ damage.

Warning signs requiring emergency care:
- Severe chest pain (possible heart attack)
- Severe headache with confusion or visual disturbance (possible stroke)
- Shortness of breath (possible heart failure)
- Back pain radiating downward (possible aortic dissection)
- Altered consciousness or seizure
- Sudden vision loss

⚠️ EMERGENCY: Call 112 immediately. Do NOT wait for symptoms to resolve on their own.

Hypertensive Urgency: Very high blood pressure (above 180/120) WITHOUT signs of organ damage. Should be treated urgently in a medical setting but is not immediately life-threatening.

--- MANAGEMENT OF HYPERTENSION ---

Lifestyle Modifications (essential for all stages):
- Reduce salt intake to less than 5 grams per day (avoid pickles, papads, processed foods)
- DASH diet: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy, lean protein
- Regular aerobic exercise: 30 minutes on most days of the week
- Weight reduction: losing 5-10kg can reduce systolic BP by 5-10 mmHg
- Limit alcohol: maximum 1 unit/day for women, 2 units/day for men
- Quit smoking
- Stress management: yoga, meditation, deep breathing

Medications (prescribed by doctor):
- ACE inhibitors: enalapril, ramipril, lisinopril
- Calcium channel blockers: amlodipine, nifedipine (commonly used in India)
- ARBs: losartan, telmisartan
- Diuretics: hydrochlorothiazide, furosemide (for fluid retention)
- Beta-blockers: atenolol, metoprolol

Monitoring:
- Home blood pressure monitoring: check BP twice daily initially
- Target BP: below 130/80 mmHg for most patients
- Below 140/90 mmHg for elderly (above 65 years)
- Regular follow-up: every 1-3 months until controlled, then every 6 months

--- STROKE ---

Stroke is a medical emergency where blood supply to the brain is cut off, causing brain damage. Hypertension is the single biggest risk factor for stroke.

Types:
- Ischaemic stroke (85%): blood clot blocking a brain artery
- Haemorrhagic stroke (15%): blood vessel ruptures in the brain

Recognize using FAST:
- F — Face drooping: Is one side of the face drooping or numb? Ask to smile.
- A — Arm weakness: Is one arm weak or numb? Ask to raise both arms.
- S — Speech difficulty: Is speech slurred or strange? Ask to repeat a phrase.
- T — Time to call 112: If any of these signs are present, call emergency immediately.

Additional symptoms:
- Sudden severe headache with no known cause
- Sudden confusion or trouble understanding
- Sudden trouble seeing in one or both eyes
- Sudden loss of balance or coordination

⚠️ EMERGENCY: Every minute of delay causes death of approximately 2 million brain cells. Treatment window for clot-busting therapy (tPA) is 4.5 hours from symptom onset.

--- HEART FAILURE ---

Heart failure occurs when the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs. Often a result of longstanding hypertension or coronary artery disease.

Symptoms:
- Shortness of breath (especially lying flat — orthopnoea)
- Waking up breathless at night (paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea)
- Swelling of ankles, feet, and legs (oedema)
- Fatigue and weakness
- Rapid or irregular heartbeat
- Reduced ability to exercise
- Persistent cough or wheezing
- Sudden weight gain from fluid retention

Management:
- ACE inhibitors or ARBs (reduce heart workload)
- Beta-blockers (slow heart rate)
- Diuretics (remove excess fluid)
- Low salt diet and fluid restriction
- Daily weight monitoring (report gain of more than 1kg/day to doctor)
- Avoid NSAIDs (worsen heart failure)

--- CHOLESTEROL AND LIPID DISORDERS ---

High LDL cholesterol is a major risk factor for heart attack and stroke.

Normal values:
- Total cholesterol: below 200 mg/dL
- LDL (bad cholesterol): below 100 mg/dL (below 70 mg/dL for high-risk patients)
- HDL (good cholesterol): above 40 mg/dL (men), above 50 mg/dL (women)
- Triglycerides: below 150 mg/dL

Symptoms: Lipid disorders are usually asymptomatic. Xanthomas (fatty deposits under skin) may appear in severe cases.

Management:
- Dietary changes: reduce saturated fats, eliminate trans fats, increase fibre
- Exercise: raises HDL cholesterol
- Statins: atorvastatin, rosuvastatin — first-line medication
- Annual lipid profile screening after age 40 or earlier if risk factors present
